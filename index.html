Framework Overview

A Three-Level Engagement Model

An employee's engagement level depends on factors at three distinct organisational levels — from how the company presents itself, through the day-to-day team experience, to the individual's personal well-being on board.

Engagement Drivers

The Five Drivers of Crew Engagement

Each driver pinpoints a domain where targeted leadership action can improve engagement. Together they explain why some crew consistently outperform while others disengage — and where to focus improvement efforts.

🏢

Company Level

Connection with Company

Crew who feel connected to the company invest more effort and are more likely to stay. This connection is shaped by how fairly compensation is perceived, trust in leadership, and alignment with the company brand. The key question: are crew meeting the minimum — or choosing to go beyond?
Salary perception Benefits Company brand Discretionary effort
👥

Team & Work Level

Relation with Manager / Officer

The nearest supervisor has a significant influence on engagement. Supervisors shape the crew's experience through commands, guidance, and feedback. Recognition for a job well done lifts engagement meaningfully — its absence has the opposite effect.
Leadership quality Guidance & clarity Recognition Feedback
⚓

Team & Work Level

Relation with Team

Strong team relations provide support and purpose on board. Good teamwork enriches the social dimension of life at sea and sustains engagement over long voyages. Being part of a cohesive crew is one of the most powerful engagement drivers in the maritime context.
Teamwork quality Peer support Social cohesion Trust
⚙️

Team & Work Level

Your Job and Daily Work

This driver covers the nature of tasks, available equipment, workload, and opportunities for skills development. Crew who view their conditions and career prospects positively are more engaged — and consistently outperform peers who lack that perspective.
Task quality Workload balance Skills development Career perspective
🩵

Individual Level

General Well-being

Living and working at sea for months is demanding. Well-being on board is shaped by the social environment, shore leave access, mental condition, and inclusiveness — including a zero-harassment culture. Crew with high well-being are far more likely to engage fully with their work.
Mental condition Shore leave Social environment Zero harassment

Key Distinction

Engagement vs. Job Satisfaction

These two concepts are related — but they are not the same. Confusing them leads to misaligned investments and interventions that improve satisfaction without driving the extra mile.

Job Satisfaction

A prerequisite — not the destination

Satisfaction is an important driver that must be met for a crew member to be fully engaged. A seafarer who is dissatisfied will rarely perform at a high level. But a content crew member may still only do the minimum required.

  • Baseline requirement for engagement
  • Reflects how crew feel about their current role
  • Necessary but not sufficient on its own
Crew Engagement

Commitment, motivation & extra effort

Engagement goes further. Engaged crew show a high drive of commitment and motivation to achieve excellent results. They invest in their work, support colleagues actively, and promote a high-performance culture across the vessel.

  • Active commitment and discretionary effort
  • Motivation to drive excellent results
  • Promotion of a high-performance safety culture

Business Case

The Value of Crew Engagement

Engaged seafarers lift the entire vessel — not just their own performance. The return on investment in crew engagement is measurable across safety, performance, and retention.

🛡️

Safer Operations

Engaged crew are motivated to do a good job and remain alert to workplace risks — translating directly into fewer incidents and a stronger on-board safety culture.

📈

Superior Performance

Engaged people considerably outperform disengaged teams. In a competitive crewing market, engagement is what separates best-practice from average operations.

🤝

Stronger Teamwork

Engaged seafarers cooperate better with colleagues and officers, actively participate in improvement initiatives, and build a crew culture that performs to a high standard.

🔁

Retention & Loyalty

Engaged crew want to stay. Turning less-engaged seafarers into high performers reduces costly turnover and retains hard-won experience in the fleet.
